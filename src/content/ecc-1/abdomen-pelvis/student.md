# POCUS Module 5 — Abdomen & Pelvis (incl. Renal & Bladder)

**Estimated completion time:** 75 minutes.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, the student will be able to:

- Perform the FAST exam and identify the dependent spaces where free fluid collects.²⁴
- Evaluate the abdominal aorta along its course and measure its diameter.²⁴
- Identify the liver and gallbladder and their key structures and variants.²⁴
- Identify renal anatomy (cortex, collecting system, hilum, vasculature) and assess for hydronephrosis.²⁴
- Perform a transabdominal pelvic scan; identify uterus, adnexa, bladder, and pouch of Douglas; estimate bladder volume.¹²⁴⁵

## Quick Reference Guide

This section is intended as a bedside reference during the hands-on scanning session.

### Probe & Preset

- **Probe:** Curvilinear (abdominal preset) for FAST, aorta, biliary, renal, pelvis; phased array for the subxiphoid cardiac view of FAST.²⁴
- **Preset:** Abdominal preset; lower frequency for penetration; full bladder as an acoustic window for the pelvis.⁴

### Key Views / Structures

- FAST: RUQ (hepatorenal/Morrison's), LUQ (splenorenal), subxiphoid cardiac, suprapubic pelvis.²⁴
- Aorta: transverse from proximal to bifurcation + long axis (measure outer-to-outer).²⁴
- Gallbladder long/short axis; kidneys bilateral long/short; bladder transverse + sagittal for volume.²⁴

### Normal Findings

- No free fluid in dependent spaces; potential spaces appear as a single bright stripe.²
- Aorta < 3 cm outer-to-outer; lies anterior to spine, left of midline, thick-walled & pulsatile.¹⁴⁵
- Gallbladder wall < ~3 mm, no stones; kidney cortex hypoechoic to liver, central echogenic sinus, no hydronephrosis.¹²⁵
- Bladder anechoic; uterus midline posterior to the bladder.²

### Common Pathology / Key Artifacts

- Hemoperitoneum: anechoic free fluid in Morrison's pouch or the pelvis.²
- AAA: aortic diameter ≥ 3 cm needs further evaluation.¹⁴⁵
- Cholelithiasis (echogenic + shadow).
- Hydronephrosis (dilated anechoic collecting system); free fluid in the pouch of Douglas.²

### Image Optimization Tips

- **Depth:** Increase depth/penetration with a low-frequency curvilinear probe; position the patient to pool fluid in the space examined.⁴
- **Gain:** Set so fluid is black and solid organs mid-gray; measure the aorta outer wall to outer wall, perpendicular to its longitudinal axis.²⁴

### Common Pitfalls

- Mistaking the IVC for the aorta (aorta = pulsatile, left; IVC = respirophasic, right).²
- Measuring the aortic lumen only (inner-to-inner) underestimates an aneurysm with thrombus.²
- Bowel gas obscuring the aorta/pelvis; mirror-image artifact above the diaphragm in the RUQ.²

### Clinical Pearl of the Day

Measure the aorta outer wall to outer wall — a lumen-only measurement misses mural thrombus and underestimates a true aneurysm.
