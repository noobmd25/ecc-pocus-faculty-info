# POCUS Module 3 — Musculoskeletal & Soft Tissue

**Estimated completion time:** 60 minutes.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, the student will be able to:

- Identify normal MSK anatomy — tendon, muscle, bone, joint, nerve, artery, and vein.¹⁴
- Scan the shoulder and knee and identify key periarticular structures.¹³⁴
- Perform a dynamic evaluation of the forearm musculature.¹³⁴
- Differentiate nerves, arteries, and veins sonographically.¹⁴
- Identify the abdominal wall layers — skin, subcutaneous tissue, fascia, muscle, peritoneum.¹⁴

## In-Session Expectations

During the hands-on session, students will:

- Handle the ultrasound probe appropriately and maintain correct orientation.⁴
- Acquire the required views with guidance.¹⁴
- Apply image-optimization principles (depth, gain, TGC, focus).⁴
- Participate in peer-assisted scanning (learner-to-instructor ratio ≤ 5:1).¹

## Quick Reference Guide

This section is intended as a bedside reference during the hands-on scanning session.

### Probe & Preset

- **Probe:** Linear high-frequency transducer; MSK preset.¹⁴
- **Preset:** MSK / small-parts preset; use copious gel or a stand-off for very superficial structures.⁴

### Key Views / Structures

- Long- and short-axis of tendons.¹⁴
- Joint recesses: elbow olecranon fossa and shoulder (biceps / supraspinatus).³⁴
- Nerve in short axis (honeycomb); abdominal wall in layered cross-section.¹⁴

### Normal Findings

- Tendon: fibrillar hyperechoic pattern (becomes dark with anisotropy if off-perpendicular).¹
- Muscle: hypoechoic with hyperechoic perimysial striations.¹
- Nerve: honeycomb / fascicular; artery pulsatile & round; vein compressible.¹
- Bone: bright continuous cortical line with clean posterior shadow.¹

### Common Pathology / Key Artifacts

- Joint effusion: anechoic/hypoechoic fluid distending a recess.¹
- Tendon tear: fiber discontinuity / hypoechoic gap.¹
- Fracture: cortical step-off or disruption.¹
- Abscess / foreign body in soft tissue.¹

### Image Optimization Tips

- **Depth:** Shallow depth; place the focus at the structure of interest.⁴
- **Gain:** Set so muscle is gray and fascia/tendon bright; keep the beam perpendicular to avoid anisotropy.¹⁴

### Common Pitfalls

- Anisotropy: a normal tendon looks black when the beam is off-perpendicular — toe/heel to confirm.¹
- Pressing too hard collapses veins and small effusions.¹
- Too much depth makes small effusions easy to miss.⁴

### Clinical Pearl of the Day

Anisotropy is the #1 MSK pitfall — a normal tendon appears black if the beam isn't perpendicular. Adjust the angle of the probe before thinking it is pathologic.
