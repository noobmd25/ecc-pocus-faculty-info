# Faculty Teaching Notes — Module 3: Musculoskeletal & Soft Tissue

This section is for instructors only and should not be released to students.

Covers the "Musculoskeletal" objectives.

## Course-Delivery Reminders

- Use a flipped-classroom approach; maximize transducer time in the trainee's hands.¹
- Have them teach you the anatomy. If you have any doubts as to what you see, please keep them for when the proctor of the session passes by the station to answer the questions, or until after the session to have the proctor answer the questions.
- Frequently rotate between students during the session for spaced repetition.¹

## Emphasize

- Emphasize anisotropy recognition before any tendon/joint interpretation.
- Emphasize the artery/vein/nerve triad as the foundation for vascular access and nerve blocks.
- Emphasize the basics of tendons, joints and cartilage, which is the foundation of any joint evaluation.

## Demonstrate

- Demonstrate anisotropy live by rocking the ultrasound probe off a normal tendon and back.¹
- Demonstrate dynamic forearm scanning during finger flexion/extension.

## Reinforce

- Reinforce that basic MSK ultrasound is great for evaluations of effusions, hematomas, tissue injuries and abscesses. The rest of the evaluations will need a specialist in this field or further studies like X-rays, or MRI to better assess joints and bones when there is concern for injury.

## Self-Assessment Answer Key

- **Q1:** Anisotropy = artifactual hypoechogenicity when the beam is not perpendicular; correct by toe-heel tilting.¹
- **Q2:** Nerve = honeycomb, non-compressible; artery = round, pulsatile, non-compressible; vein = compressible, respirophasic.¹
- **Q3:** Skin → subcutaneous fat → fascia → muscle → peritoneum.¹⁴
- **Q4:** Elbow olecranon fossa; shoulder subacromial-subdeltoid / tendon in bicipital groove.⁴
- **Q5:** Detailed elbow/shoulder joint protocols and dynamic forearm exams (beyond EM-core MSK).
