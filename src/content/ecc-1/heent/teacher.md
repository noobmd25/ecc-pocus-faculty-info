# Faculty Teaching Notes — Module 2: HEENT — Ocular, Neck & Vascular

This section is for instructors only and should not be released to students.

## Course-Delivery Reminders

- Use a flipped-classroom approach; maximize transducer time in the trainee's hands.¹
- Have them teach you the anatomy. If you have any doubts as to what you see, please keep them for when the proctor of the session passes by the station to answer the questions.
- Frequently rotate between students during the session for spaced repetition.¹

## Emphasize

- Emphasize ocular safety (ensure ocular setting is used, no globe compression) as a hard rule.¹
- Emphasize the compression maneuver as the core skill that unifies vein identification and DVT.¹
- Remember: red or blue in color Doppler is not venous or arterial flow — it is flow toward (red) or away from (blue) the transducer.
- The key difference in Doppler is the pulsatile behavior of the arterial flow compared to the vein.

## Demonstrate

- Demonstrate the gentle "eyelid-only" technique with abundant gel and minimum to no pressure.¹
- Demonstrate vein-vs-artery differentiation — compressibility ± color Doppler (pulsatility).¹⁵

## Reinforce

- Reinforce that thyroid, carotid stenosis, and detailed nodal work-up are consultative, NOT basic-core knowledge — teach as anatomy/skills, refer for diagnosis.¹
