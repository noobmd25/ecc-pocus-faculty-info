# POCUS Module 4 — Cardiothoracic: Cardiac (FoCUS) & Respiratory

**Estimated completion time:** 60 minutes.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, the student will be able to:

- Obtain the five core cardiac views — parasternal long (PLAX) & short axis (PSAX), apical 4-chamber, subxiphoid, and IVC.¹⁴
- Identify the pericardium, four chambers, valves, and great vessels; estimate qualitative LV function.¹⁴
- Assess the IVC for size and collapsibility to estimate central venous pressure.¹⁴
- Identify A-lines, B-lines, and lung sliding.¹⁴
- Assess diaphragm motion (M-mode) and diaphragmatic excursion by dynamic evaluation.¹³⁴

During the hands-on session, students will:

- Handle the ultrasound probe appropriately and maintain correct orientation.⁴
- Acquire the required views with guidance.¹⁴
- Apply image-optimization principles (depth, gain, TGC, focus).⁴
- Participate in peer-assisted scanning (learner-to-instructor ratio ≤ 5:1).¹

## Quick Reference Guide

This section is intended as a bedside reference during the hands-on scanning session.

### Probe & Preset

- **Probe:** Phased array for cardiac, IVC, and diaphragm; phased or curvilinear for lung; linear for pleural detail.¹⁴
- **Preset:** Cardiac preset for the heart/IVC; abdominal or lung preset for the chest.⁴

### Key Views / Structures

- PLAX, PSAX, Apical 4-chamber, Subxiphoid, IVC long axis.¹⁴
- Anterior and lateral lung fields, each hemithorax (sliding, A-/B-lines).¹⁴
- Subcostal diaphragm with M-mode for excursion.³⁴

### Normal Findings

- Pericardium: single bright line, no surrounding fluid; LV walls thicken symmetrically.¹
- IVC < ~2.1 cm with > 50% inspiratory collapse suggests normal/low CVP.⁴⁶
- A-lines (horizontal reverberation) + lung sliding = normal aeration.¹
- Diaphragm excursion > ~1 cm with quiet breathing.³⁴⁶

### Common Pathology / Key Artifacts

- Pericardial effusion / tamponade (anechoic rim, RV diastolic collapse).¹
- B-lines ≥ 3 per field → interstitial syndrome (CHF, pneumonia, ARDS).¹
- Absent lung sliding → possible pneumothorax (confirm with lung point).¹
- Anechoic collection above the diaphragm → pleural effusion.¹

### Image Optimization Tips

- **Depth:** Cardiac: fit the whole heart in view; decrease depth for IVC; for lung, bring the pleural line up to assess sliding.⁴
- **Gain:** Set so blood pools appear black and myocardium mid-gray; apply BART convention for color Doppler (Blue Away, Red Toward).¹⁴

### Common Pitfalls

- Foreshortened apical view underestimates chamber size/function.¹
- Descending thoracic aorta behind the pericardium can mimic effusion.¹
- Calling "B-lines" without ≥ 3 in a field; epicardial fat-pad mimicking effusion.¹

### Clinical Pearl of the Day

On PLAX, the descending thoracic aorta sits behind the bright pericardium — fluid anterior to it is pericardial, fluid posterior to it is pleural.
