# POCUS Module 1 — Foundations: Physics, Instrumentation & Knobology

This module provides the required preparatory content for this semester's hands-on ultrasound sessions.

**Estimated completion time:** 45–60 minutes.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, the student will be able to:

- Explain the ultrasound physics relevant to POCUS — piezoelectric effect, frequency, resolution, attenuation, echogenicity, and Doppler.¹⁴
- Operate the system to optimize the image using depth, gain, time-gain compensation (TGC), and focus.¹⁴
- Select the appropriate transducer for the clinical question and compare resolution vs. penetration trade-offs.¹⁴
- Identify common artifacts — reverberation, shadowing, enhancement, mirror, side-lobe, and ring-down.¹⁴
- Apply safety principles (ALARA, thermal index, mechanical index) and recognize the limitations of POCUS.¹³⁴

## Quick Reference Guide

This section is intended as a bedside reference during the hands-on scanning session.

### Probe & Preset

- **Probe:** All three: Linear (high-frequency, superficial), Curvilinear (abdominal, deep), Phased array (cardiac, small footprint).¹⁴
- **Preset:** Match preset to region (e.g., Abdominal, Cardiac, MSK, Ocular, Vascular).⁴
- **Butterfly note:** Our course uses a single Butterfly probe — one physical transducer that switches between linear, curvilinear, and phased array modes in the app, instead of swapping probes. Cart-based machines you will use later on typically have three separate physical probes that you must physically exchange to change preset/transducer type.

### Key Views / Structures

- Probe orientation marker ↔ on-screen indicator (establish before scanning).⁴
- Long-axis vs. short-axis imaging planes.⁴
- Manipulation: slide, fan/tilt, rock, rotate, compress.⁴

### Normal Findings

- Anechoic (black) = simple fluid; hyperechoic (white) = bone/air/calcium interface.¹
- Marker dot on the probe corresponds to the indicator on the screen.⁴

### Common Pathology / Key Artifacts

- Posterior acoustic shadowing → stone, bone, or calcification.¹
- Posterior acoustic enhancement → fluid-filled structure behind.¹
- Reverberation / A-lines, ring-down (comet-tail), and mirror-image artifacts.¹

### Image Optimization Tips

- **Depth:** Set so the target organ fills roughly two-thirds of the screen — not too deep, not cropped.⁴
- **Gain:** Adjust so simple fluid appears black and soft tissue appears mid-gray; use TGC for depth-dependent attenuation.¹⁴

### Common Pitfalls

- Excess gain → false internal echoes in fluid (can mimic or hide pathology).⁴
- Wrong preset or wrong probe for the depth → poor resolution or penetration.⁴
- Ignoring the orientation marker → left/right or near/far reversal.⁴

### Clinical Pearl of the Day

High frequency = high resolution but shallow penetration; low frequency = deep penetration but lower resolution. Match the probe to the depth of your target.

## Self-Assessment

**1. You need to image a shallow structure just under the skin. Which type of transducer is generally the best choice?**\
A) Low-frequency probe, for deeper penetration\
B) High-frequency probe, for better resolution at shallow depths\
C) Any probe, since frequency doesn't affect image quality\
D) A probe with the largest footprint available

*Answer: B*

**2. You are scanning a fluid-filled structure but see unexpected bright speckled echoes inside it. What should you check first?**\
A) The depth setting\
B) The gain setting\
C) The screen brightness of your monitor\
D) The patient's position

*Answer: B*

**3. A structure deep to a fluid-filled area appears artificially brighter than surrounding tissue. What is this artifact called?**\
A) Shadowing\
B) Mirror image artifact\
C) Posterior acoustic enhancement\
D) Reverberation artifact

*Answer: C*

**4. What does the ALARA principle in ultrasound safety stand for?**\
A) Always Limit Any Radiologic Assessment\
B) As Low As Reasonably Achievable\
C) Adjust Levels According to Resolution and Amplitude\
D) Avoid Long Applications, Reduce Artifacts

*Answer: B*

**5. Which situation best represents a limitation of point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS)?**\
A) It can be performed at the bedside\
B) It provides real-time imaging\
C) A finding is inconclusive and additional imaging is needed to confirm a diagnosis\
D) It doesn't use ionizing radiation

*Answer: C*
